Canon Support Code 5B00 Explained Without the Jargon

Few Canon messages cause as much confusion as "Support Code 5B00." It shows up on PIXMA and MegaTank printers alike, the printer goes silent, and the message on screen tells you only to contact a service center. Here is what is going on in plain terms.

The short version. 5B00 (and its close relative 5B01) means the printer's ink absorber is full, or at least the printer believes it is. Each time the printer cleans its print head, it pushes a little ink out and down into a set of absorbent pads. Canon tracks that volume, and when the estimate reaches its limit the printer stops to protect your desk from leaking ink.

How it looks on different models. Printers with a two-character display usually show P07 or a flashing light pattern alongside 5B00. Models without a display signal it with alternating Alarm and Power lamp flashes. Some printers warn you earlier with an "absorber almost full" message, often support code 1700 or 1701.

What will not fix it:

  • Restarting or unplugging the printer
  • Running head cleaning or deep cleaning (this makes the problem worse)
  • Reinstalling the driver or changing cartridges

What will:

  1. A service center can replace the absorber pads and reset the counter.
  2. For an out-of-warranty printer, some owners replace the pads themselves or fit an external waste tank, then reset the counter.
  3. If the printer is old and the repair costs more than its value, retiring it is a reasonable choice.

Prevention. Avoid back-to-back cleaning cycles. Use the nozzle check first and clean only when it shows missing lines. Printing a little each week keeps the nozzles from clogging, which means fewer cleanings overall.
